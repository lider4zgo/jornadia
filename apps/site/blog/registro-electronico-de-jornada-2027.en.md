Starting January 1, 2027, employers in Mexico will have to record each worker’s hours electronically. If you track attendance today with a paper sheet, a spreadsheet or WhatsApp messages, these weeks are the time to get ready. Here is what the reform says, what is still pending, and what you can do now.

## What the reform says

On May 1, 2026, the decree amending Mexico’s Federal Labor Law (Ley Federal del Trabajo) to reduce working hours took effect. Among other changes, it added section XXXIV to article 132, which requires employers to:

> “Electronically record the working hours of each worker, including start and end times, and provide them to the authority when requested.” (unofficial translation)

In other words: every clock-in and clock-out must be stored electronically, and you must be able to hand the records over if the labor authority asks for them.

## When it applies

The decree says the Ministry of Labor (STPS) will issue the general rules that define who the obligation applies to and its exceptions, and that those rules take effect on **January 1, 2027**. May through December 2026 works, in practice, as an adjustment period for employers and workers.

As of this writing (November 13, 2026), we have not found those STPS rules published. That doesn’t change the date: it makes sense to prepare with what the law already says and adjust the details once the rules are out.

## The workweek goes down to 40 hours

The same reform gradually lowers the maximum workweek and raises the overtime allowed. Shorter hours cannot reduce wages or benefits.

| Year | Maximum hours per week | Overtime allowed per week |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 | 48 h | 9 h |
| 2027 | 46 h | 9 h |
| 2028 | 44 h | 10 h |
| 2029 | 42 h | 11 h |
| 2030 | 40 h | 12 h |

That is why the record matters: without it, it’s hard to know how many hours each person actually worked and whether they passed the limit.

## Overtime: how it’s paid

- Overtime within the weekly limit is paid at **double** the regular wage.
- Hours beyond that limit are paid at **triple**.
- It can be spread over up to 4 hours a day, on at most 4 days a week.
- Regular plus overtime hours can never exceed **12 hours a day**.

## Fines

Not keeping the electronic record can cost **250 to 5,000 UMA** (article 994, section IV Bis). The UMA is Mexico’s reference unit for fines.

## The record as evidence

The law says the content of the electronic record is **full proof** if it is shown that the worker and the employer agreed to it. A well-kept record, agreed in writing, can be your best support in a labor dispute. Specialists recommend that the agreement state the medium used, the data recorded, how errors are corrected and how workers can check their own records.

## How to get ready in 6 steps

1. **List your staff and their schedules.** Who works at each branch or site, on which shift and with which rest days.
2. **Review your current overtime.** Compare what your team works today against 46 hours in 2027 and the 9 overtime hours allowed per week.
3. **Choose a digital time clock.** A system that records each person’s clock-in and clock-out and stores the data electronically (see what to look for below).
4. **Agree on the record in writing with each worker.** In the contract or a separate document: the medium used, the data recorded and how errors are corrected. That way the record can serve as full proof.
5. **Run a trial before January.** A few weeks of use will show signal problems, misconfigured shifts or staff who don’t know how to clock in.
6. **Share the reports with your accountant.** Recorded overtime has to reach payroll complete.

## What to look for in a digital time clock

So far, the law only requires the record to be electronic and does not name a specific device: it can be an electronic time clock, an app or a web platform. While the STPS issues its rules, look for a system that:

- **Identifies each person** when they clock in (for example, with a PIN or photo) and stores the date and time of entry and exit.
- **Leaves a trail of every correction**: who made it, when and why. A record that can be changed without a trace loses value as evidence.
- **Works where your people are**: on site, on the road or at a branch, even without signal.
- **Calculates hours worked and overtime** against each year’s limit.
- **Lets you export the records** to hand them to the authority or your accountant, and lets workers see their own.
- **Protects personal data**: if it uses photos or facial recognition, it asks for the worker’s consent and offers an alternative without biometrics.
